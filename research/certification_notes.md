## Main mathematical additions

1. **Projected acceleration error theorem without Lipschitz projected complement.** Define G_p as the L2 closure of compact smooth gradients and epsilon_t=||Pi_p a_t||. Retain globally Lipschitz original acceleration a, but require no regularity at all for w=a−Pi_p a. For H=max step,
   - E_H=exp(L_a H^2/2)
   - B_H=(1+LH)E_H
   - D_H=C_j/6+E_H(LC_a/2+H L_a C_a/8)
   - max W2 <= exp(LT)[D_H sum h_j^3 +(B_H/2) sum h_j^2 epsilon(t_j)].

   The new quantitative ingredient is W2(Phi_a^tau#p,p) <= [(exp(L_a tau)−1)/L_a]||Pi_p a||. Proof uses a continuity-equation path mu_s=Phi_a^(tau−s)#p with velocity −D Phi_a^(tau−s)Pi_p a. No auxiliary flow for w is required.

2. **Exact local Wasserstein coefficient.** Under the projected theorem assumptions, at every fixed t<T, lim_{h->0} W2(Euler-from-p_t,p_{t+h})/h^2 = epsilon_t/2. Upper bound is the new one-step estimate; lower follows by Taylor expansion of any compact smooth test in an optimal coupling, with gradient norm measured under the Euler law converging to its target norm. This strengthens zero-residual local necessity to an exact quantitative local characterization. It does not forbid terminal cancellation. No density, projected regularity, or invertibility of Euler is used.

3. **Primal/dual certificate.** For any q in gradient closure and exact weighted-solenoidal w, L=2<a,q>−||q||^2 <= epsilon^2 <= U=||a−w||^2, with exact gap U−L=||a−w−q||^2. Finite Galerkin projection norms are lower bounds, not upper certificates. This is an elementary standard Hilbert-space duality identity; do not claim its abstract form novel.

4. **Poisson and approximation certificates.** Poincare permits a finite-energy weak potential and the upper epsilon^2 <= ||q_N||^2+C_P||p^−1 div[p(a−q_N)]||^2 for an exact Galerkin solution. A separate bracket accounts for quadrature error and L2 acceleration approximation. These are delimited statements, not claims that a practical Monte Carlo standard error is a deterministic bound.

5. **Compactly supported gauge search.** Positive C4 target p, finite C4 stream potentials supported in one compact K, regular base v, compact parameter set. Global regularity holds uniformly; objective sup_t ||Pi_p a_theta|| is Lipschitz and attains a minimum. Exhaustive delta-net search with certified objective brackets of width eta achieves minimum+K delta+eta. No guarantee for arbitrary local optimization or neural training. Compact support avoids 1/p tail blowup. A time envelope uses the material derivative of an upper residual along the target field.

6. **All diagnostics under the target density.** For target p generated by v and integrated u, beta_t=||Pi_p(u−v)||. With mixed jerk bound sup_{t<=s}||j_u(s,F^u_{t,s}(.))||_L2(p_t), max W2(Euler_u,p) <= exp(LT)[initial W2 + cubic sum + projected-acceleration quadratic sum + integral beta]. This avoids incorrectly plugging target p into a theorem about an unknown learned q. Uniform linear growth of jerk is a sufficient mixed-moment condition. Population conditional-flow-matching excess loss gives integral beta <= sqrt(T R_excess). Exact target-solenoidal gauges leave beta unchanged.

7. **Density/score mismatch.** For r=tilde_p^−1 J grad psi, div(p r)/p = r dot(score_p−score_tilde_p); with target Poincare C_P, ||Pi_p r|| <= sqrt(C_P)||r dot score_error||. This quantifies path defect of an approximate-density gauge.

## Audit notes

- Independent adversarial check by the feasibility-theory agent verified the frozen-flow CE sign and assumptions, local residual constants, target comparison, and exact local coefficient proof. One time-envelope exposition gap was corrected by explicitly requiring a C1 upper-certificate field and material derivative bound.
- Core projection exists for arbitrary finite-second-moment p, without Poincare or density; potential representation has additional explicit hypotheses.
- Derivatives in frozen-flow proof use globally Lipschitz C1 a; test pullbacks are compactly supported C1, and smooth approximation justifies weighted-divergence testing.
- Temporal projections admit Borel versions through a countable dense smooth-gradient basis and finite projections; no ODE for the rough projection is used.
- The mixed jerk bound in the target theorem is essential. Merely controlling jerk under the field's own law does not justify Taylor estimates from target starts.
- Nodes need actual residual upper bounds; a time-integrated residual or a finite training grid alone is insufficient.
- This extension improves the usability/assumptions of the existing residual theorem. It is not a claim of a general exact correction or end-to-end speedup.
- Existing appendix discussion near the end of the residual proof says weighted projection does not supply the regularity needed by the old proposition. That statement remains true for the old proposition, but should now point to the new theorem as the remedy.
